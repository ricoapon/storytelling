# Lost Friendship

At the start of this year, I made a friend. Her name is Clara, and we met through salsa.

From day one, we clicked. Sometimes when I meet somebody, I can have this feeling and I know: we are gonna be friends. I had that with Clara. We exchanged numbers, we started texting, and before you know it we were texting every day. We saw each other a few times a week. We went to salsa parties together all the time. I would come home late, and still call her for half an hour before going to bed. Sometimes she would come over, and we would lie on the couch, hugging and watching Harry Potter (her favorite movie).

She became my best friend.

And it was only that, a friend. But the way we interacted is not something this society considers a "normal" friendship. Calling and texting every day, laying in the couch together hugging each other watching a movie, that is stuff couples do. I live my life however I want and not by how society thinks I should live it, but I am aware of how others were perceiving our friendship. She was too. Both of us had friends that were telling us it looked like a relationship but without the sex. And we had a lot of conversations about it as well. What are our boundaries? How do we feel for each other? We were both very communicative and I liked how well we approached our friendship. Anything that would bother us would be discussed. People always say "Communication is key". I know it is cliché, but I really believe in it. Clara did too. I think the reason our friendship worked so well is _because_ we talked about everything. Communication **IS** important.

Clara was very clear about one thing: she was not romantically interested in me. I didn't know at the time how I felt for her. I felt a beautiful connection, she did too, and that made me very happy. That was good enough for me.

There is a tiny little detail that was also holding back my own feelings: she is 11 years younger than me. I am 33 and she is 22. That is huuuuuuge gap in age. I understand how that comes across. What I found interesting is how she felt a lot older than her age. She is genuinely emotionally mature. I would very often be impressed by how well she could articulate her emotions, thoughts and opinions in a way that people my age rarely do. We could have this very serious and interesting conversation, and then I would look at her face. And I would just burst out laughing haha, because she looks so young! She hated that.

Over time, I realized I did fall in love with her. There was a very specific moment I realized this. There was this one week when she wasn't doing well, a lot of stuff happened in one week, unrelated to me, and she wanted some time alone. She went to her mother in Belgium. We were not in touch for a week. And man, did I miss her in that week. I was so used to being in touch with her every single day. Talking to her when I wake up, talking to her about my day when I would go to bed. That dissapeared and it had a huge impact on me. And that scared me. She affected me in such a strong way. She was clear in not wanting anything romantic. I realized it was time to take a step back from the friendship.

So I did what I always do: I told her about it. When she recovered and came back to the Netherlands, she came over. I told her how I felt and that I wanted to take a step back. She thought that was a good idea, and we talked about what we would do differently. I didn't want to end the friendship, neither did she, but just maybe not text every day. Or call before going to bed. After the conversation, we still hung out for a bit. Then, as usual, I walked her to the bus stop and waited with her until her bus arrived. We hugged, she got on her bus and I turned around to walk away. And then... it hit me... I suddenly felt very heavy... I felt sad... There was this one very clear thought that came to me... The friendship... was done... My whole body felt it... There was no way around it. I could not continue the friendship. I went home and I cried. I just talked with Clara about taking a little bit of distance, but I knew I needed more distance. I don't mean Clara had to disappear from my life forever. I mean that particular closeness was gone. The daily texting, hugging on the couch, being there for each other. I could not keep living in something that felt like a relationship, when in reality it was not a relationship. Maybe, after enough distance, a new kind of friendship could grow. But the old connection was gone.

The next week, I did not text her at all. Obviously, she knew something was up. And so again, I did what I always do: I told her about it. We called and I told her I needed even more distance. She was incredibly understanding. She said she believed our friendship would be okay because our connection was strong. She told me to take all the time I needed. That she said that to me was such a relief. I felt like I could really focus on myself and not worry about how Clara would feel. I could take all the time in the world and really work through my own feelings.

I tried. But man, life was not kind to me. August was a terrible month for me. A family member died. Dates went terribly. I became very sick. And on top of all of that, I couldn't talk to the one person that I wanted to talk to.Luckily, other friends were there for me. Eventually, I became fit and happy again.

And then... I ran into Clara at a salsa party. I was like yeah, I feel happy. Let's talk to her again. See what happens. And she did not want to talk to me. She didn't want to dance with me. I didn't understand why. So I asked her. Turns out, she was angry. She told me I had said I wanted a short break, and then I had not spoken to her for a month. I was shocked. She had told me to take all the time I needed. She said to me: "I have thought about it, had many realizations and I don't want to have you in my life anymore."

...

That... broke me... For weeks, I did not sleep well. After that terrible month, the hope of a new friendship had kept me going. Now, even that was gone. I asked whether we could talk it through: what were those realizations? What had I done? She did not want to. I had this feeling that I did something terribly wrong. Only I had no clue what. And she wouldn't tell me. The friendship... was really done... The price we pay for love... is grief.

...

What did life want to teach me here? That is the question I asked myself. I looked back on the friendship and how it developed. And I am proud on the person I was in that friendship. There is an expression I really like: the only person you have to impress is yourself. I am impressed by how well I communicated. I am impressed with how in touch I am with my feelings. I am impressed by how I can stay vulnerable, even if the other person does not.

There is also a lesson to be learned here. Maybe, just maybe, I should not try to date twenty-two-year-olds.
